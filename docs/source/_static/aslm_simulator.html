    How the simulator works, and its assumptions

    Waveforms are generated exactly as in mesoSPIM/src/utils/waveforms.py: the ETL is a tunable_lens_ramp (delay / rise / fall in % of sweeptime, amplitude = half peak-to-peak), the galvo is scipy.signal.sawtooth with duty cycle and phase, and the laser and camera trigger are single_pulses. The camera starts line 0 at the rising edge of the trigger; each following group of lines per step starts one line interval later and is exposed for camera_exposure_time.

    Light sheet: Gaussian beam with Rayleigh range zR = Lls/2 (Lls in sample space), so w0 = √(zRλ/πn). At every time step the waist sits where the ETL puts it; the beam radius at line x is w(x) = w0√(1+(x−xwaist)²/zR²) and the excitation at depth z is (w0/w)·exp(−2z²/w²) (energy conserved). All thicknesses on this page are FWHM = 1.1774·w0 (w0 = 1/e² radius). The ETL follows its command through a first-order lag (time constant τ) — this is why exposure should start ~10% into the ramp. Galvo: the beam is swept along y; the time it spends in each pixel row during each line's exposure window is integrated exactly, so stripes appear when the exposure is not an integer number of galvo half-periods.

    Sample: a stack of layers at z = 0, ±3, ±7, ±14, ±25, ±45, ±80, ±140 µm with cell bodies and dim autofluorescence; filaments are smooth 3D curves through ±200 µm that drift in and out of the focal plane at shallow angles (up to the set maximum tilt), so a thin sheet shows short in-focus segments and a thick sheet shows long hazy ones. Each layer is blurred by the detection PSF plus geometric defocus (detection NA). The image is the sum over layers of (layer image × exposure-integrated excitation of that layer), plus optional shot and read noise. So a thick effective sheet shows up as out-of-focus structures and haze, not as a lateral blur.

    Calibration: the ETL calibration is fixed in the model (2500 µm/V, waist at FOV centre at 2.5 V), so ETL voltages here are relative — use the Fit buttons rather than copying them to or from a config. The galvo calibration (µm/V, centre voltage) and ETL τ stand in for what is measured on the instrument. Fixed, not shown: detection NA 0.15 (phantom blur, depth of field), sample rate 100 kHz, camera trigger pulse 1%, line-interval limits from the sensor preset. Not modelled: camera trigger latency, sensor-specific line-time quantisation (e.g. Iris 15: 10.26 µs × (scan_line_delay+1)), galvo/ETL non-linearity, scattering, pivoting, second arm.

    Rules of thumb from the notes: virtual slit Nslit = texp/dtL lines should match the light sheet, Nslit·dxpx ≈ Lls·M; galvo half-period × n = texp; readout texp + (Npx−1)·dtL ≈ 0.9 × ETL ramp; narrower slit ⇒ longer line interval ⇒ slower frame rate but better axial resolution. Reference: Liu et al. 2021 (CompassLSM), Fig. S3.
